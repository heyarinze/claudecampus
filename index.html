01 — About

About Claude Campus Fellows

The Claude Campus Fellows program is a community of graduate students, primarily from the humanities, who convene and guide campus-wide conversations on AI and society for both graduate and undergraduate students.

Claude@Campus
Builders

Campus Ambassadors

  • Campus Ambassadors lead the Claude Builder Clubs on their campuses.
  • They act as technical product evangelists for Claude across student communities.
Thinkers

Campus Fellows

  • Campus Fellows lead the Claude Thinker Salons on their campuses.
  • They serve as non-technical thought curators for conversations on AI and society.

Program objectives

  1. Give non-technical students a voice in steering AI development and its impact on society.
  2. Increase Claude usage among students from the humanities, arts, and culture disciplines.
  3. Help Anthropic’s teams understand how students are using Claude for personal and professional use cases.
02 — Structure

Program & Thinker Salon Structure

Each design choice is deliberate. Tap any card to see the rationale behind it.

01

1 Campus Fellow per campus

A single fellow per campus solves the bystander effect that emerges when multiple fellows share responsibility but engage unequally.
02

7–10 students per salon

A group this size ensures depth of engagement for each participant rather than letting voices fade into the background.
03

3–4 disciplines per salon

Mixing disciplines captures a wider surface area of student insights and prevents salons from collapsing into a single intellectual register.
04

1:1 grad : undergrad ratio

An even split between graduate and undergraduate students gives equal voice to participants across educational levels.
05

10-week program

A 10-week length lines up with the timeline of the sister Campus Ambassador program, so both efforts move in lockstep on each campus.
06

Pilot: 5–10 priority schools

The pilot will focus on schools with an existing Claude for Education presence or deep involvement in AI ethics, such as Oxford’s Schwarzman Center for the Humanities and its Institute for AI Ethics.

Starting small gives the program team a learning window to refine the model before scaling to more campuses.

07

Faculty sponsor required

A faculty sponsor provides institutional accountability and keeps salon topics and published artifacts aligned with school priorities and policies.

“For Ambassadors, the work is distribution. For Fellows, the work is curation.”

04 — Positioning

Program Positioning

How Claude Campus Fellows fits within Anthropic’s DNA — and how it diverges from every other AI campus program.

Built and run by both builders and thinkers

Anthropic builds frontier AI systems while stewarding thoughtful conversations on AI and human flourishing — and the Campus program is designed to mirror that duality.

Founder alignment

The company is co-led by builder and thinker archetypes — Dario’s engineering background and Daniela’s humanities background — so its Campus Program needs to mirror that same duality on every campus.

Org + mission alignment

As one of the few AI labs structured as a Public Benefit Corporation rather than a typical C-Corp, Anthropic has a legal mandate to balance frontier development with thoughtful deployment. Campus Ambassadors carry the former; Campus Fellows carry the latter.

Builders Campus Ambassadors

Campus Ambassadors carry the frontier-development side of Anthropic’s mandate, driving Claude’s adoption among student builders.

Thinkers Campus Fellows

Campus Fellows carry the thoughtful-deployment side, hosting discourse on AI and society and modeling responsible use.

How Campus Fellows is different

Most AI campus programs optimize for usage, growth, and product demos. Claude Campus Fellows focuses on critical thinking, salons, and ongoing student-led artifacts rather than one-off materials.

Others

Perplexity’s Campus Partner and Lovable’s Campus Leaders programs are usage- and growth-centric. OpenAI’s “100 Chats for College Students” produced a single, centrally authored book, and its ChatGPT Lab initiatives are centrally organized rather than student-led.

Claude Campus Fellows

A stream of student-led artifacts from ongoing salons across global campuses — designed for depth, plurality, and societal reflection over short-term growth metrics.

Core Insight

  • Most AI campus programs focus primarily on “builders,” leaving humanities and non-technical students on the periphery.
  • Historically, big questions about technology and society are answered when sciences and arts work together.
  • Focusing only on technical communities effectively cuts the total addressable student market in half.

Claude Campus Fellows unlocks the other half.

05 — Resources & Dependencies

Resources & Dependencies

What the program needs to run — and the risks it must actively manage.

Resources
Conversation Guides
Instructionals with prompts, facilitation tips, and ideas on using Claude to synthesize salon insights.
Employee Time
~1 hour per month aggregated from education, beneficial deployments, research, and policy teams for Campus Fellow Seminars, where FTEs share “big questions” and selected Fellows share campus insights.
Fellow Compensation
Equal to Campus Ambassador compensation, reinforcing equal standing as co-leaders of the Claude community on campus.
Salon Reimbursement
Reimbursement for salon-related expenses up to $500 per term, mirroring the Builder Club budget.
Content Distribution Channels

An owned channel (e.g. YouTube or Substack) for showcasing high-quality artifacts and insights from student salons.

Note: any artifacts amplified on Anthropic channels will go through a light editorial and safety review aligned with Anthropic content standards.

Recruitment Pipelines
A signal into research-adjacent roles that value humanities and social-science expertise — Claude for Education, Beneficial Deployments, Policy/Society teams, and for hybrid profiles, research engineer/scientist tracks.
Dependencies & Mitigations
Reputation Risk

Risk: content produced by Fellows could be misinterpreted as Anthropic-approved.

Mitigation: mandatory disclaimer — “This content, curated by a Claude Campus Fellow, represents the personal views of the students in the salon, not Anthropic’s views or those of its employees.” Violation results in removal from the program.
Program & Title Conflation

Risk: confusion between Campus Fellows and Anthropic FTE fellowship programs — similar to the Campus Ambassador vs. Community Ambassador dynamic.

Mitigation: clear guidelines for how Fellows list their role on LinkedIn and other platforms; monitored by the Campus program team; violations result in removal.
Research Depth Confusion

Risk: salon artifacts being mistaken for fully researched, market-shaping insights due to the Claude/Anthropic halo.

Mitigation: required disclaimer that artifacts represent insights from guided conversations, not in-depth empirical research.
06 — Success Metrics

Success Metrics

How we’ll know the program is working — and which signals matter most to students versus Anthropic.

Emphasize:

Primary Goals

  • 01
    Salons organized per campus iA steady cadence of salons ensures Claude and Anthropic maintain an active, visible presence on each campus. Anthropic value
  • 02
    Interdisciplinary diversity per salon iThis signal measures how far Claude is reaching into non-technical disciplines beyond the usual builder audience. Student value
  • 03
    Claude artifacts created per salon iRequired artifacts confirm that students are actively using Claude to synthesize discussions and reflect on what came up. Anthropic value
  • 04
    Reach of artifacts & salon content iTracked through podcast plays, essay reads, and zine downloads, this expands how society thinks about use cases for Claude artifacts and creative tools like Claude Design. Student value
  • 05
    Product / program / policy decisions influenced by student insights iWhen salon insights show up in real decisions, students gain measurable agency in how Anthropic builds and ships. Anthropic value
    For example, Anthropic employees may quote student insights in internal and external presentations, and Claude for Education resources may explicitly build on salon artifacts.

Aspirational Goals

  • 06
    FTE hires from the Fellowship iEvery Fellow who later joins as an FTE signals the high quality bar of the program and the long-term talent value it produces. Anthropic value

Global · Fellowship

These experiences give me a tested playbook for recruiting, facilitating, and sustaining small, high‑trust thinking salons across very different contexts and markets.
